Welcome to the Classic Salkantay Trek!
Before embarking on this adventure, it’s important to prepare your gear

weather is very variable, with cold temperatures in the early morning and
at night, heavy rains, and intense sun during midday. But don’t worry, with
the right equipment, you can enjoy the hike without any problems.
Take note and get ready for this adventure!

Sleeping bag
Nights in the Andes can be freezing, reaching temperatures below zero.
Therefore, we recommend bringing a sleeping bag with thermal insulation
of -15°C to keep you warm. Don’t worry about carrying it, we will carry it
for you to the campsites.

We recommend bringing a sleeping bag with thermal
insulation of -15°C.

Walking sticks
Walking sticks are an essential tool in your hiking gear, as they help
to cushion the load and maintain balance while trekking through the
mountains. They are beneficial for protecting knees on uneven terrain in
the Andes. While not mandatory, many hikers prefer them for the comfort
they provide.

Water bottle or Camelback
Staying hydrated during the hike is crucial. We will provide filtered water
during the trip. Therefore, it is necessary to bring a container to store
and drink water. The Camelbak is a handy item for drinking comfortably
without having to unpack.

Flashlight
When the sun begins to set in the afternoon or during your stay at the
campsites, it is essential to have a flashlight on hand to illuminate if
you need to look for something among your belongings or go to the
bathroom. Headlamp flashlights are efficient, as they allow you to keep
your hands free.

What should I wear on the trek?
Upper Garments
Jacket
Choosing the right jacket is essential to enjoy
the Salkantay experience fully. To choose the
right jacket to wear, we suggest the following:
Temperatures in the Andes can drop
below 0 °C. Choose a jacket with thermal
insulation to keep you warm.
Precipitation in the Peruvian Andes is
unpredictable and abundant. Therefore,
it is recommended that your jacket be
waterproof and have a hood to protect you
from the rain.
Choose a jacket with multiple pockets with
zippers to store objects and protect them
from the rain.
Some passengers bring a lightweight
waterproof jacket or windbreaker to feel
light during the hike and, separately, a
thick and warm jacket for colder moments.
Others prefer a single jacket that combines
thermal and waterproof protection. The
choice is yours.

In addition to a jacket, we recommend bringing the following garments
for the upper body:

Warm Jacket

Long Sleeved T-Shirt

Raincoat

To protect yourself from the cold, it’s
advisable to wear warm, comfortable,
lightweight, and breathable
garments such as fleeces or sweaters.

Keep in mind that in some areas of
the hike, especially in warmer ones,
there may be mosquitoes, so wearing
a long-sleeved shirt is recommended.

Also, bringing a rain poncho can be
very useful in case of rain. Make sure it’s
waterproof and that you can cover your
backpack too.

Lower Garments
Pants
To walk comfortably, choosing pants with the right features is
important. Consider the climate in the Andes before making your
decision.
Choose synthetic pants that dry quickly, are flexible and
resistant, provide freedom of movement, and have smooth
seams to avoid chafing on the skin. It’s recommended that they
have several pockets.
We recommend wearing waterproof pants during the rainy
season (from September to March).
In addition, you should consider the low temperatures. You
can opt for thermally insulated pants or mesh-type pants
underneath to keep you warm in the coldest stretches of the
hike.

Accessories
Protect yourself from the sun
During the hike, it’s important to protect yourself from the sun, especially
during noon. Therefore, you should bring the following:

Hat

Sunglasses

Bring a hat, preferably with
protection for your neck and
face, and choose breathable
and lightweight materials

Protect your eyes from the sun’s rays
with sunglasses with adequate UV
protection.

Protect yourself from the cold
The weather in the Andes can be freezing, especially at night.
To keep yourself warm, we recommend bringing the following
accessories:

Wool hat

Gloves

Buff

During the coldest moments of the
hike, keeping your head and ears
covered is essential. We recommend
bringing a wool hat.

Your hands can feel the cold wind of
the Andes. Wearing gloves will help
protect them from the cold.

We also suggest bringing a bandana,
scarf, or buff, to avoid the cold air and
dust.

What shoes to choose?
We recommend wearing specific hiking shoes
with the following characteristics:
Flexible, thick sole with good grip

Lightweight footwear or sandals
After a day of hiking, your feet may feel tired and need a
break. For greater comfort at the campsite, we recommend
bringing sandals or lightweight footwear to change shoes
and relax your feet.

Comfortable and supportive for the foot
Waterproof
